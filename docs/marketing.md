# Argus
### Marketing Plan

Version 1.0

---

## 1. Positioning

Argus is the remote control for developers running AI coding agents. It is not a coding assistant and not another agent. It is the layer that lets someone run several agents at once, leave the desk, and stay in control from a phone.

One line: run your agents, walk away, stay in control.

## 2. Who this is for, in order

1. Developers already running OpenCode (or similar CLI agents) who juggle multiple terminal windows and lose track of what is happening
2. Indie hackers and freelancers running several client projects through AI agents at once
3. Small dev teams and agencies, later, once the solo-developer experience is proven

Do not market to teams yet. The product is not built for them, and early messaging that promises team features before they exist damages trust.

## 3. Messaging pillars

- **Stop babysitting terminals.** One dashboard for every agent, every project, every status.
- **Walk away without losing control.** Real notifications, real remote answers, not a static log you check later.
- **Never get blindsided by quota.** See usage before it runs out mid task.
- **Built by a developer who hit this problem, not a company selling infrastructure.** Honest, technical, no hype.

## 4. Channels, ranked by cost and fit

All of these are free or near-free. No paid ads in the early phase, there is no budget for acquisition that doesn't compound.

1. **Build in public.** Post progress, screenshots, and short demos as the product is built. This is the highest-leverage, zero-cost channel for a technical audience, and it builds an audience before launch instead of after.
2. **Communities where the target user already is.** Discord and forums for OpenCode, Claude Code, and similar CLI agent tools. Answer questions, be useful first, mention the product only when it is genuinely relevant.
3. **Hacker News (Show HN) and r/programming, r/selfhosted, r/LocalLLaMA.** One well-prepared Show HN post at launch, not a drip of low-effort posts.
4. **X / developer Twitter.** Short demo clips (agent needing input, phone getting the notification, responding, agent continuing) perform far better than text-only updates.
5. **Product Hunt launch**, once the PWA is stable enough for strangers to try without hand-holding.
6. **YouTube devlogs**, optional, only if you enjoy making them. A single good "I built a remote control for my AI agents" video can outperform months of text posts, but only start this if it is sustainable alongside building.

## 5. Content strategy

Show the loop, not the architecture. The most persuasive content is a 20 to 40 second clip of: agent hits a decision point, phone buzzes, you tap an answer from your phone, agent keeps going. Everything else (the tech stack, the Go service, the adapter pattern) is interesting to a subset of the audience and belongs in longer-form devlog posts, not the primary hook.

Repeat the same core demo across every channel with the framing adjusted: HN wants the technical why, Twitter wants the 20 second clip, Reddit wants an honest "here's what I built and why" post.

## 6. Launch sequence

1. **Private beta**, 10 to 20 people from the communities in section 4.2, recruited by being visibly useful there first. Goal: find the reliability problems before strangers do.
2. **Public waitlist**, opened alongside the last few weeks of beta, so there is a list to email on launch day.
3. **Launch day**, Show HN post plus Product Hunt plus a Twitter thread, all pointing at the same landing page and the same core demo clip.
4. **Post-launch**, weekly build-in-public updates, responding to every piece of feedback publicly where it makes sense, since that is itself marketing to anyone watching.

## 7. Budget

Zero paid acquisition spend at launch. The entire plan runs on time and consistency, not money. The one expense worth budgeting for, once there is early traction, is a small amount (well under $100) for a domain and, if native mobile is ever built, the Apple developer fee.

## 8. What to avoid

- Do not promise team features, marketplace, or advanced analytics before they exist. The audience for this product is technical and will notice the gap immediately.
- Do not buy ads before organic channels have been tried and measured. A technical, narrow audience like this one is usually cheaper and more responsive to reached through community presence than through paid acquisition.
- Do not launch on every channel at once before the product has been used daily by a handful of real people. A broken first impression in a developer community is hard to undo.
